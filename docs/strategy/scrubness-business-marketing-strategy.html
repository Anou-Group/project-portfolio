Summary

Where we are

Scrubness sells colourful scrub tops and pants through its own WooCommerce store and through Southern Uniforms. The live catalogue has 84 products and 842 size variants. A full site backup has been taken, and supplier barcodes are prepared for upload.

What is recommended

Fix the product data and the GST display first, switch on product reviews, start an email list, and list the range on Google Shopping. Then run a small, capped advertising test focused on scrub pants, which have the most search interest.

Committed cost

Current running costs (hosting, domain, plugin licences) are not documented in the records I reviewed and are listed as pending confirmation.

Decisions needed

Confirm how retail prices treat GST, the brand's owner and decision-maker, approval of the proposed test budget, and a review of trademark-style colour names.

4. Target customers and market positioning

Pending confirmation The intended customer is not stated on the website. From the product range and search data, the likely customers are:

  • Individual healthcare and allied-health workers (nurses, aged-care and disability support workers, vet nurses, dental staff) buying their own scrubs.
  • Clinics, practices and aged-care providers buying team sets through wholesale or through Southern Uniforms.

Confirmed Keyword research (Google Trends, Australia, 12 months to 25 Oct 2026):

  • "Scrub pants" is the strongest product term, with about twice the interest of "scrub tops" and about three times "nurse scrubs".
  • Rising searches include wide-leg scrub pants, cute or colourful scrub tops, and seasonal prints (Halloween, Christmas).
  • Brands that appear alongside these searches include FIGS, Mandala, Gorman, Dangerfield, Kmart, Big W and Cotton On.

Proposal Position Scrubness as colourful, comfortable, size-inclusive scrubs at an accessible price. The range runs to women's size 30 and 5XL, has 4–9 pockets per garment, and retails at $49.95–$54.95 ex GST. This is a clear point of difference from both mass-market and premium scrub brands. Competitor prices have not been reviewed and should be checked before price-based claims are made.

8. Initial digital marketing strategy

None of the activities below are running. They are a proposed plan, to be approved before any spend.

Search engine optimisation (SEO)

  • Proposal Make scrub pants the lead landing page, with copy that uses customers' own search terms ("scrub pants", "scrub pants women", "black scrub pants").
  • Proposal Write unique product descriptions that cover fabric, pockets and fit (drawn from the price list) and add size-range keywords ("sizes 6–30", "up to 5XL").
  • Proposal Plan seasonal collections ahead of search peaks (Halloween and Christmas prints) where stock allows.

Google Ads

  • Proposal Google Shopping on scrub pants and best-selling tops once barcodes are live, plus a small brand-search campaign so people searching "Scrubness" land on the store.

Social media, Instagram and Facebook

  • Pending confirmation Current posting activity, follower numbers and who manages the accounts.
  • Proposal Instagram-led content on four themes: colour drops, real healthcare workers in Scrubness (with permission), size-inclusive fit, and pocket and fabric details. Two to three posts a week, shared to Facebook.
  • Proposal Remove the empty Twitter link or replace it with an active account.

Meta Ads

  • Proposal Retarget site visitors first, then test new audiences with healthcare-job interests, using colour and fit creative. Stay within the capped test budget.

Content marketing

  • Proposal A size and fit guide, a "which scrub is right for you" comparison (classic or stretch fabric; pocket counts), and care instructions. Each piece links to products.

Customer acquisition

  • Proposal Email capture with a welcome message, then new-colour and restock announcements.
  • Proposal Wholesale outreach to clinics, vet practices and aged-care providers with the 2026 price list, coordinated with Southern Uniforms so the two businesses do not approach the same customers separately.

Risks to address

  • Proposal Some colour names refer to well-known brands or characters (for example "Vegemite", "Barbie", "Shrek" and "Tiffany"). I recommend a trademark check before these names are used in advertising.
  • Proposal The website describes the fabric as antimicrobial. This should be supported by supplier documentation before it is used in ads.
